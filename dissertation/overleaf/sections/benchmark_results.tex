\section{Algorithm benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark-results}

Nine labelled datasets provide an algorithm check before the customer analysis, using the label-free selection procedure and the internal indices defined in \S\ref{sec:candidates}. Six shape datasets and three multivariate datasets were evaluated with K Means, CLASSIX, and, for this benchmark only, DBSCAN and Ward's method (defined in \S\ref{sec:benchmark-robustness}). Selection used only internal indices and deployment guards; reference labels entered only the subsequent ARI and NMI calculation, after selection, and never influenced which candidate was chosen.

\textbf{Result.} No method dominates the benchmark. K Means has the highest mean ARI across the nine selected results at 0.597, followed by Ward at 0.575, DBSCAN at 0.509 and CLASSIX at 0.508. CLASSIX and Ward share a mean rank of 2.44, while K Means has 2.39 and DBSCAN has 2.72. These averages conceal marked dependence on shape: CLASSIX recovers the Jain reference exactly but reaches only 0.264 ARI on R15; DBSCAN recovers Spiral exactly and performs best on Aggregation and Compound, yet its selected Wine candidate has no valid internal score; K Means is strongest on Seeds and Wine, while Ward is strongest on R15.

\textbf{Mechanism and interpretation.} Each method's strengths track its own structural assumptions: CLASSIX's radius-based aggregation suits compact, well-separated shapes like Jain but is a poor match for R15's many overlapping Gaussian clusters; DBSCAN's density criterion suits Spiral's connected, non-convex arcs; K Means's convex Voronoi cells (\S\ref{sec:kmeans-math}) suit the roughly spherical clusters in Seeds and Wine. No single method wins because no single geometric assumption fits every dataset, and this dependence on shape — not any one method's general superiority — is the benchmark's main finding.

\textbf{Limitation.} This pattern of successes and failures shows that the four implementations, the label-free selector, and the external evaluation code all behave sensibly across a deliberately varied set of known structures; it is a check on the analytical pipeline, not evidence about which method is preferable for RetailRocket customers, whose true cluster shape (if any) is unknown. The customer conclusion in \S\ref{sec:paired-results} onward rests on paired evidence within one cohort, not on this benchmark's algorithm ranking.

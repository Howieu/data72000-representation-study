\section{Sensitivity and robustness}
\label{sec:sensitivity}

This section answers RQ5 (\S\ref{sec:intro-rq}): do the representation effects reported in \S\ref{sec:paired-results}--\S\ref{sec:explanation-results} survive specific, named alternative explanations, or are they artefacts of one parameter choice? Each diagnostic below targets one such alternative explanation and is read as ruling that specific alternative in or out — none of them, individually or together, is a full variance decomposition of the compact-to-rich effect. Fixed checks (feature deletion, PCA, perturbation, session windows) measure local dependence around the already-selected configuration. Reselection repeats the candidate search and deployment rule from scratch after changing the features. The permutation control tests what happens when added dimensions carry no customer-aligned information at all.

\subsection{Named features and reduced geometry}
\label{sec:sensitivity-features}

\textbf{Result.} Fixed parameter deletion identifies category behaviour as the strongest local influence on the rich partitions. Removing distinct top categories reduces agreement with the full rich result to ARI 0.189 for CLASSIX and 0.516 for K Means. Removing top category share gives ARI 0.238 and 0.517. Every other CLASSIX deletion remains between 0.927 and 0.967. The corresponding K Means values remain between 0.855 and 0.991. Activity span and mean interevent time have the next largest effect on K Means, with ARI 0.874 and 0.855.

\textbf{Interpretation.} This rules out one specific alternative explanation of the rich-representation effect: that it is spread roughly evenly across all nine added variables. Instead, influence is concentrated: removing either of the two category variables alone moves the rich CLASSIX partition roughly as far from its own full-feature result as the two representations are from each other cross-representation (\S\ref{sec:paired-results}), while removing any of the other seven added variables leaves CLASSIX largely unchanged. Category information is therefore a specific, identifiable driver of the observed rich-representation structure, not an unattributable side effect of adding "more dimensions" in general.

\textbf{Limitation.} Influence is unevenly distributed across the named variables, but this does not mean category information alone would generate a \emph{preferable} segmentation, nor does removing one variable at a time capture interaction effects between variables removed together.

The PCA diagnostic preserves 91.32 per cent of standardised rich variance in six components. It is a fixed-parameter local diagnostic rather than PCA-space model selection. K Means in this reduced space agrees with the named rich partition at ARI 0.995 and has silhouette 0.390 compared with 0.351. CLASSIX retains three clusters, lowers noise from 218 to 71 customers and agrees with the named result at ARI 0.920. Its silhouette is 0.304 compared with 0.291. Mean resampling ARI is 0.995 for reduced K Means and 0.987 for reduced CLASSIX. The principal component space preserves most fitted structure. It is not used for explanation because rotated components do not retain the direct business meaning of the twelve named variables.

Complete reselection confirms that category information creates the largest structural turn. All twenty two scenarios produce a deployment acceptable candidate. Removing distinct top categories changes CLASSIX to eleven clusters with ARI 0.153 against the full rich partition. K Means changes to two clusters with ARI 0.728. Removing the whole category block produces seven CLASSIX clusters with ARI 0.126 and two K Means clusters with ARI 0.729. The progressive endpoints reproduce the compact and rich primary partitions exactly. These 4,334 candidate fits show that the category effect persists when parameters and cluster counts are allowed to change.

CLASSIX sensitivity is distributed across the grid rather than concentrated at one radius. Deployment acceptable candidates occur at twenty three of thirty one compact radii from 0.35 to 1.45 and twenty rich radii from 0.30 to 1.25. Minimum size 1 produces no acceptable candidate in either representation. Minimum sizes 5 and 10 produce 23 and 32 acceptable compact candidates, compared with 19 and 27 rich candidates.

\subsection{Perturbation and temporal assumptions}
\label{sec:sensitivity-perturbation}

\textbf{Result.} Small Gaussian perturbations leave the selected CLASSIX partitions almost unchanged. Compact agreement remains ARI 1 through a noise standard deviation of 0.001. At 0.01 the mean ARI is 0.999 and the minimum is 0.996. Rich agreement is also exact through 0.0001. It falls to a mean of 0.995 at 0.001 and 0.985 at 0.01, with minima of 0.985 and 0.962.

\textbf{Interpretation.} This rules out a second specific alternative explanation: that the low cross-representation agreement reported in \S\ref{sec:paired-results} is an artefact of numerical instability rather than a genuine geometric effect of the added variables. If the fitted partitions were fragile to tiny numerical noise, that fragility — not representation — could explain why compact and rich results differ. Instead, both partitions are essentially exact under noise far smaller than the perturbation needed to move any customer between clusters; the richer fit is measurably more sensitive at the largest tested noise level, consistent with \S\ref{sec:kmeans-math}--\S\ref{sec:classix-math}'s point that more dimensions contribute more terms to every distance calculation, but it does not become unstable in the sense of wholesale reassignment.

\textbf{Limitation.} These empirical results do not certify the density-merging branch used by the rich CLASSIX selection (Table~\ref{tab:customer_results}); the mathematical certificate in \S\ref{sec:stability-certificate} applies only to distance merging when its stated margins hold, and is a proof under stated conditions, not a description of what was empirically observed here.

\textbf{Result.} Reconstructing sessions from raw events gives a similar qualified result. The selected thirty minute window reproduces both partitions exactly. A fifteen minute window gives ARI 0.976 for K Means and 0.971 for CLASSIX. A sixty minute window gives ARI 0.985 and 0.959. The four-cluster K Means result and the three-cluster CLASSIX result persist at both alternative windows, although the CLASSIX noise count changes from 218 to 220 and 239.

\textbf{Interpretation and limitation.} This rules out a third alternative explanation: that the main findings depend on the specific choice of a thirty-minute inactivity gap. They do not, at the level of cluster count and overall structure. The detailed customer-level assignments and exact noise count, however, still depend on the session convention, so this robustness is qualified rather than absolute.

\subsection{Dimensionality control}
\label{sec:sensitivity-dimensionality}

\textbf{Result.} The permutation control sets a stricter boundary on causal interpretation. The compact columns remain unchanged while nine additional dimensions, independently permuted across customers, are appended. These permuted columns preserve their own marginal distributions but destroy their joint relationship with the compact representation and with each other, by construction. With the compact K Means setting held fixed, adding all nine permuted columns lowers agreement with the compact partition to ARI 0.017 and silhouette to 0.127. With the compact CLASSIX setting held fixed, adding even one permuted dimension produces a single cluster and ARI 0.

\textbf{Interpretation.} This demonstrates that added dimensionality and standardisation \emph{can}, by themselves, substantially alter both methods' fitted geometry, even for columns that are constructed to carry no genuine information about any customer — direct evidence for the scaling-sensitivity mechanism described in \S\ref{sec:kmeans-math}.

\textbf{Limitation.} This is a single deterministic existence demonstration, not a variance decomposition, and it must not be read as one. It shows \emph{that} dimensionality alone can move geometry; it does not estimate \emph{how much} of the observed, genuine compact-to-rich difference (\S\ref{sec:paired-results}) is attributable to dimensionality as opposed to the specific behavioural content of the nine real added variables. Named feature deletion (\S\ref{sec:sensitivity-features}) and PCA (above) separately show that category and temporal information specifically, not dimensionality in the abstract, contribute reproducible structure in the observed rich representation — so the representation effect reported throughout this dissertation should be attributed to what the added variables measure, not merely to how many of them there are.

\section{Integrated discussion and validity limits}
\label{sec:integrated-discussion}

The study concerns purchasers recorded in one anonymous event log. It excludes visitors without a transaction event and has no demographic, price, revenue, campaign or outcome data. Category features cover 76.16 per cent of events, while 1,168 purchasers have no known category. Zero ratios can indicate a genuine absence of views or the limits of recorded behaviour. These boundaries prevent substantive labels such as valuable, loyal or profitable from being inferred from the clusters.

Internal indices and fixed-parameter overlap-resampling measure geometric separation and conditional repeatability within the observed design. They do not establish an objectively correct number of customer groups or stability of complete model selection. The deployment constraints prevent nearly single-cluster solutions from dominating selection, but the thresholds are operational choices rather than universal standards. The nine labelled benchmarks provide algorithm checks across several shapes and dimensions, not a guarantee that their relative performance transfers to customer data.

Explanation evidence has separate limits. CLASSIX provenance is exact for its own fitted labels but does not produce a short business rule. ExKMC fidelity measures agreement with a K Means predictor and does not validate that predictor. The addressability hierarchy introduced in \S\ref{sec:addressability-hierarchy} checks size, coverage and observable distinctiveness, yet none of the selected CLASSIX clusters passes it, and only one of eight selected K Means/CLASSIX clusters overall does. Business value here means the capacity to inspect provenance, export named conditions and audit coverage — the fifth link in that hierarchy. Intervention response, commercial return and human understanding, its sixth link, require prospective evaluation beyond the available data and are not claimed anywhere in this dissertation.

\section{Representation and customer cluster structure}

Table~\ref{tab:customer_results} compares independently selected deployment-acceptable fits. K Means selects four clusters in each space, but their agreement is 0.014 ARI. CLASSIX selects two compact and three rich nonnoise clusters; its 218 noise assignments are purchasers not allocated to a retained cluster under density merging. Cross-representation ARI is 0.068. These values compare two fitted assignments on the same purchasers, not accuracy against known customer types.

Internal indices disagree because they weight separation and dispersion differently. Compact CLASSIX has silhouette 0.595, Davies Bouldin 1.057 and Calinski Harabasz 4285.5; rich values are 0.291, 0.859 and 1516.9. Davies Bouldin favours rich, while the other indices favour compact; each uses its partition's nonnoise subset. Compact K Means has silhouette 0.560 and Calinski Harabasz 10459.1, compared with 0.351 and 3923.4 for rich, while Davies Bouldin rises from 0.775 to 1.040. No criterion establishes a representation-independent quality ranking.

Silhouette alone selects nearly undivided populations. Compact CLASSIX reaches 0.899 with 11,698 of 11,719 customers in one cluster; rich CLASSIX reaches 0.792 with 11,716 in one cluster and three across two tiny clusters. Compact K Means reaches 0.850 with 11,593 in one of two clusters. Isolating distant outliers can give the majority high silhouette without useful subdivision. All three fail the 95 per cent maximum-share rule, an operational choice rather than a mathematical law. Reporting both optima exposes this tradeoff. Only rich K Means has identical unconstrained and deployment selections.

Low agreement is not caused only by choosing different numbers of clusters. At every fixed K from 2 to 12, the K Means ARI between compact and rich representations remains between 0.012 and 0.113. At the common selected value of four it is 0.014, with NMI 0.089 and variation of information 1.691 nats. Under matched labels, 54.61 per cent of customers move to a different K Means cluster. Among 41 CLASSIX settings that are deployment acceptable in both spaces, matched-parameter ARI ranges from $-0.051$ to $0.392$ with a median of $-0.023$. The independently selected CLASSIX partitions have NMI 0.054 and variation of information 0.693 nats, with 18.66 per cent assigned to a different matched cluster or noise state. The lower CLASSIX migration share partly reflects the dominance of one large cluster in both representations and should not be read as stronger substantive agreement.

Cluster sizes make the changes more concrete. Compact K Means contains 7,784, 2,494, 1,344 and 97 customers. Rich K Means contains 6,790, 3,607, 1,156 and 166. Its 166 customer cluster has a median of 338.5 total events, 28 sessions and 12 top categories, whereas the full cohort medians are 6 events, 2 sessions and 1 top category. Another rich cluster of 1,156 customers has no known category at its median and a purchase recency median of 127.4 days. These are descriptive contrasts in observed features, not customer identities or causal segments.

CLASSIX is more imbalanced: compact cluster sizes are 10,656 and 1,063; rich sizes are 10,331, 1,137 and 33, plus 218 noise assignments. The 33-customer group has medians of 869 total events, 36 sessions and 14 top categories. Rich features thus expose unusual activity and missing-category groups, but operational assessment below shows why these need not be usable segments.

Additional standardised coordinates change distances, K Means centres and CLASSIX sorting/neighbourhood decisions. Resampling retains one representation while changing fitted purchasers; cross-space comparison changes geometry. High repeatability and low cross-space agreement are therefore compatible, without identifying a causal share attributable to individual features.

Mean overlap-resampling ARI is 0.985/0.965 for compact/rich CLASSIX and 0.988/0.995 for K Means. The lowest of ten CLASSIX values is 0.881/0.941. Each selected configuration usually reproduces its grouping when one fifth of customers is omitted. This is conditional repeatability, not proof of unique correctness or stable model selection.

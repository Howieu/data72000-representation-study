\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Research background}

Customer segmentation groups people by recorded characteristics, including purchases, browsing and other interactions. Customers need not be purchasers; this study concerns only visitors with a recorded purchase in RetailRocket.

A clustering algorithm receives numerical feature vectors. The \emph{representation} is the choice and construction of these features; their \emph{geometry} consists of distances and neighbourhood relationships between observations. Purchase summaries and additional browsing or category measures can make the same people appear similar in different ways. Comparing algorithms without controlling these choices can obscure why partitions differ.

A \emph{partition} assigns observations to groups, allowing a separate noise state where a method leaves observations unassigned. Clusterability concerns whether data contain structure suitable for clustering, but no definition applies to every structure \citep{ackerman2009clusterability,adolfsson2019clusterability}. The present study therefore examines observed grouping under a specified representation and evaluation design.

Explanation introduces a second distinction. CLASSIX forms and merges local groups; its \emph{provenance} records the route from an observation through those groups to its final cluster \citep{chen2024classix}. K Means assigns observations to centres, while ExKMC subsequently builds threshold rules approximating those assignments \citep{frost2020exkmc}. A construction record and a rule approximation answer different questions; neither alone establishes usefulness or human understanding.

\section{Research questions and contribution}

The comparison holds the 11,719 purchasers, event snapshot, common purchase variables, candidate grids and evaluation rules fixed. Rich features retain the three compact features and add nine behavioural measures. The research addresses:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The effect of representation on customer assignments, geometric quality and repeatability for K Means and CLASSIX.
\item The effect on CLASSIX provenance, ExKMC accuracy at a given rule budget, and compliance with operational checks.
\item Sensitivity to feature removal, reduced dimension, numerical changes and alternative session definitions.
\end{enumerate}

The contribution links representation, clustering, explanation and operational assessment in one paired design. It separates within-space repeatability from between-space agreement and intrinsic provenance from post hoc approximation. Mathematical analysis explains CLASSIX projection pruning and local sufficient conditions for an unchanged distance-merging fit. It does not prove general CLASSIX stability or identify objectively correct customer types.

\section{Dissertation structure}

Chapter 2 positions the study in the literature; Chapter 3 defines its data, methods and mathematical scope. Chapter 4 begins with labelled benchmarks, including DBSCAN, which joins dense regions and can leave sparse observations unassigned, and Ward's method, which merges groups to limit increases in within-group squared dispersion \citep{ester1996dbscan,ward1963hierarchical}. Labels evaluate benchmark results but do not select parameters. Customer comparisons then address the three research questions without treating either fitted partition as ground truth. Chapter 5 draws a bounded conclusion.

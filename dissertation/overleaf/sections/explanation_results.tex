\section{Explanation complexity, fidelity and addressability}

CLASSIX provenance describes its own construction; ExKMC fidelity evaluates approximation of K Means labels. Their values are not combined into one explanation score.

Compact CLASSIX produces 131 aggregation groups and two final clusters; rich produces 559 groups, three clusters and noise. Each customer trace records the visitor, group, representative and final label. Both exports reproduce selected labels exactly with ARI 1. The larger group count means finer computational provenance, not measured reading time or cognitive difficulty.

At four leaves, both representations have four rules, depth 3 and 2.25 conditions per rule. Compact training/test fidelity is 0.9466/0.9448; rich is 0.9639/0.9650. Across five splits, test fidelity ranges from 0.9416 to 0.9480 and 0.9599 to 0.9697 respectively. These are descriptive ranges, not inferential uncertainty; the training/test proximity gives no sign of a training-only gain.

Compact rules use purchase occasion count and purchase recency across all five splits. Rich rules use purchase occasion count, activity span and the number of distinct top categories. Thresholds are exported in original units after reversing the training scaler and logarithmic transform. Named thresholds support direct database style conditions, but they inherit the meaning and missingness limits of the constructed variables.

At sixteen leaves, compact fidelity reaches 0.9924 with mean depth 6.6 and 4.84 conditions per rule; rich reaches 0.9747 with depth 6.4 and 4.74 conditions. Rich begins higher at four leaves, but compact benefits more from additional cuts and overtakes it. Representation thus changes the observed budget--fidelity relationship, without establishing minimum possible complexity or human interpretability. Training-only K selection chooses two compact and four rich clusters in every split. Its compact fidelity is 0.9985 with two rules, but differing K excludes it from the main complexity comparison.

The addressability audit asks a further question beyond rule export. A cluster can exist without being well separated, be repeatable without having short rules, and have short rules without adequate size or feature coverage. Passing those operational checks still does not establish business value. These properties require separate evidence. Neither compact CLASSIX cluster passes because neither differs from the cohort median on at least two named features by the required half interquartile range. In the rich result, the 33-customer cluster is too small, the 1,137-customer cluster has zero category coverage, and the 10,331-customer cluster lacks two sufficiently distinctive median conditions. None of the five nonnoise CLASSIX clusters is classified as addressable. No compact K Means cluster passes. One of four rich K Means clusters passes all gates, while the others fail because they have too many distinguishing conditions or insufficient category coverage.

The one passing cluster meets a technical audit, not an outcome test. Queryability and coverage do not establish campaign effectiveness, revenue or human understanding.

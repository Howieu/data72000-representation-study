Customer segmentation depends on the information used to describe each customer, yet representation is often treated as neutral. This dissertation compares a compact three-variable transaction representation with a nested twelve-variable behavioural representation for the same 11,719 RetailRocket purchasers, holding the cohort and evaluation design constant throughout. Two clustering methods are used: K Means, a standard centroid-based method, and CLASSIX, a faster method that also leaves behind its own traceable record of how each customer was grouped (its \emph{provenance}). A separate technique, ExKMC, is used only afterwards to approximate the K Means groupings with simple rules.

Changing the representation produces markedly different customer groupings. Comparing the two independently chosen K Means partitions to each other gives an adjusted Rand index (a standard 0-to-1 agreement score between two groupings of the same people) of only 0.014; the two CLASSIX partitions agree at 0.068. Both scores are close to what unrelated random groupings would give by chance. At the same time, refitting each representation on repeated 80 per cent subsamples reproduces its own grouping with mean agreement above 0.964. A representation can therefore be highly self-consistent while still disagreeing sharply with the other representation of the same customers.

The explanation tradeoff also changes with representation. Approximating the K Means groupings with a four-rule decision tree reaches 0.9448 held-out accuracy in the compact space and 0.9650 in the richer space; CLASSIX's own traceable record grows from 131 to 559 intermediate groups when the richer representation is used. Passing a documented, size- and coverage-based audit of whether a cluster could realistically be targeted with a simple business rule, only one of the four richer-representation K Means clusters succeeds, and no compact-representation or CLASSIX cluster does.

Representation therefore changes observable cluster structure and the conditions needed to explain it. Richer behavioural information does not automatically improve internal cluster quality or produce deployment-ready segments; its demonstrated value here is limited to descriptive and queryable structure, pending prospective evidence that such segments respond differently to any actual business intervention.

# Terminology audit

Method: for every term on the task specification's checklist, the current dissertation source (`dissertation/overleaf/sections/*.tex`, read in the order the compiled chapters appear — Introduction, Literature Review, Data and Methodology, Results and Analysis, Conclusion) was searched for its first occurrence, and that occurrence was checked for whether a definition, gloss, or citation-with-explanation accompanies it. "First use" is judged by reading order in the compiled PDF, not alphabetically or by file name.

Verdict key: **Defined** (a working definition is given at or near first use) · **Partial** (a citation or partial gloss is given, but the working meaning a reader needs is not stated) · **Undefined-before-use** (the term is used, sometimes with a specific number attached, before any explanation is available to the reader).

## Overall pattern

Fifteen of the twenty-one audited terms are **Undefined-before-use** or only **Partial** at their first appearance; only "resampling stability", "eigengap" (symbol-level), and "Hungarian matching" have an adequate — if minimal — definition close to first use. This distribution is consistent with, and independently confirms, the supervisor's headline complaint that "technical terms are used without being properly defined" (task specification §1) and the nine terminology-specific annotations in `revision/supervisor_feedback_audit.md` (SF-01, SF-04, SF-07, SF-09, SF-10, SF-11, SF-12, SF-15, SF-16).

The recommended fixes above are deliberately concise (one clause to one sentence each) per the task specification's instruction not to turn the dissertation into a textbook; they add a working definition sufficient for how the term is *used in this dissertation*, not a general tutorial.
